Figure 1. Study flow and timing of follow-up echocardiography. A, study flow. Left (blue), derivation cohort (index admissions 2018–2024; 2021 was excluded because in-hospital echocardiogram reports were missing from the export). Right (red), temporal validation cohort: of the 1,232 index admissions in 2025–2026, the 1,103 whose first in-hospital echocardiogram was on or after March 8, 2025 (data to September 23, 2026); 107 had their first in-hospital echocardiogram earlier and 22 had none. Uncomplicated course: maximal coronary Z score below 2 on every in-hospital echocardiogram report. Each later step includes children in whom every echocardiogram in the earlier windows was normal (Z < 2) and who had an echocardiogram in the next window; windows are counted from discharge. Finding: coronary involvement (Z ≥ 2) on any echocardiogram in the window (derivation, 1–30 days: 35 children at the first echocardiogram and 4 at a later one). Validation children were required to have 30, 90, or 365 days of potential follow-up for the respective windows; the window after 365 days was not assessed in the validation cohort. B, timing of the first to fourth echocardiograms after discharge in children of the derivation cohort with an uncomplicated in-hospital course and at least 1 echocardiogram after discharge (n = 1,885; echocardiograms to February 28, 2025; several reports on one day counted once). Boxes, interquartile range; dark line, median; horizontal lines, 10th to 90th percentiles; logarithmic axis. Dashed lines, visits scheduled about 1 week and 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after discharge (labels at the top). Right, children with that echocardiogram and the median (IQR) number of days after discharge.

Figure 2. Findings at successive echocardiograms after discharge in children with an uncomplicated in-hospital course. Blue, derivation cohort (2018–2024); red, temporal validation cohort (2025–2026). A, coronary artery aneurysm (maximal Z ≥ 2.5) and B, any coronary involvement (maximal Z ≥ 2) by window after discharge. The 1–30-day window counts the first echocardiogram after discharge; each later window includes children in whom every echocardiogram in the earlier windows was normal and who had an echocardiogram in that window, and counts the largest finding in it. Bars show percentages, whiskers Wilson 95% CIs, and labels children with the finding/children examined; NA, not assessed (validation follow-up too short). Validation against derivation, 2-sided beta-binomial predictive test: aneurysm 1–30 days, P = 0.33; any involvement 1–30 days, P = 0.68; aneurysm 31–90 days, P = 1.00; and any involvement 31–90 days, P = 1.00 (this comparison was not prespecified). Medium or larger aneurysm (Z ≥ 5) at the first echocardiogram: derivation 11/1,618 (0.68%), validation 0/615 (P = 0.049). C, cumulative incidence of a newly detected aneurysm after discharge (1 − Kaplan–Meier) in children with an uncomplicated course and at least 1 echocardiogram after discharge (derivation n = 1,885, 34 aneurysms within 730 days; validation n = 686, 6 aneurysms). Time 0 is discharge; an aneurysm is dated at the first echocardiogram that showed it, so the early rise partly reflects the timing of the first follow-up visit; follow-up is censored at the last echocardiogram (derivation also at 730 days). Shaded bands, 95% CIs. Derivation: 1.7% (1.2–2.4) at 30 days, 2.1% (1.5–3.1) at 1 year, and 2.4% (1.6–3.6) at 2 years. Validation (curve added after the validation plan was locked): all 6 aneurysms within 12 days of discharge; 1.0% (0.4–2.1) at 1 year (39 children still followed).

Figure 3. Robustness of the aneurysm yield at the first echocardiogram after discharge, and the course of the aneurysms found. A, aneurysm (maximal Z ≥ 2.5) at the first echocardiogram 1–30 days after an uncomplicated in-hospital course in the primary analysis and in alternative analyses (blue, derivation; red, temporal validation). Whiskers, 95% CIs (Wilson; bootstrap for the weighted and standardized estimates); dashed line, the primary estimate. Alternative analyses: baseline defined by the last in-hospital report only; first echocardiogram within 60 days; children treated with IVIG; readmissions within 30 days excluded; first echocardiogram on illness days 11–21; index admissions 2018–2020 or 2022–2024; 2021 with a rule-based in-hospital baseline from the discharge record, alone and pooled with 2018–2024; events counted only from reports with exact numbers; all index years 2018–2026 (except 2021) and all echocardiograms, with the wording model for reports without numbers; the 1,221 children without an echocardiogram 1–30 days after discharge handled by inverse probability weighting, by standardization to the risk-factor distribution of all 2,839 children, or by assuming that none had an aneurysm; stricter event definitions (Z ≥ 2.5 again on the next echocardiogram within 90 days, also counting children not reimaged; exact maximal Z ≥ 3.0); in the 276 children whose in-hospital maximum was an exact Z score, a rise over that maximum at least as large as the repeatability coefficient (SD-based, prespecified; robust, post hoc); and, in the validation cohort, the category assigned without the conclusion rule for non-proximal segments or with the derivation-era classification. B, aneurysms expected from measurement noise alone among the 276 children whose in-hospital maximum was an exact Z score, if each child's true Z had stayed at that maximum, under 4 estimates of the within-child SD from consecutive reports more than 30 days after discharge (later pairs) or 1–3 days apart in hospital (in-hospital pairs); the SD-based estimates were prespecified and the robust estimates (median absolute deviation) were added post hoc because the differences were heavy-tailed. Dashed line, the 8 aneurysms observed; probability of at least 8 under noise alone, in the order shown: 1.00, 1.00, 0.089, 0.35. C, aneurysms found at the first echocardiogram 1–30 days after an uncomplicated in-hospital course: AHA category at detection and at the last report more than 30 days after discharge (derivation, median 237 days after discharge; echocardiograms to February 28, 2025). Medium or larger aneurysm at any later echocardiogram in the derivation cohort: 5 children. No involvement, Z < 2; small aneurysm, Z 2.5 to <5; medium, Z 5 to <10; large or giant, Z ≥ 10 or internal diameter ≥ 8 mm; no report after 30 d, no echocardiogram more than 30 days after discharge. AHA, American Heart Association; echo, echocardiogram; IVIG, intravenous immunoglobulin.

Figure 4. When coronary findings were seen, and the course of in-hospital aneurysms (derivation cohort). A and B, coronary status by day of illness: A, children with an in-hospital echocardiogram; B, children with an uncomplicated in-hospital course (Z < 2 on every in-hospital report), in whom every finding was first seen after discharge (medium or larger aneurysms are not shown because they were too few). Only children with a recorded date of fever onset contribute. Each child contributes the largest finding among the echocardiograms in each illness-day window; percentages are of the children examined in that window (numbers under the axes). Of first-documented involvement (n = 440) and aneurysms (n = 331) with a recorded onset date, 94.8% and 94.6% had been recorded by illness day 21, and 97.5% and 98.2% by day 42. C, children with each finding at any time (any involvement n = 614, aneurysm n = 477, and medium or larger n = 189) whose finding was first documented 1–30 days or more than 30 days after discharge; the remainder were first documented in hospital (561, 432, and 153 children; for 1 of the 153 medium or larger aneurysms, on an echocardiogram before admission). Whiskers, Wilson 95% CIs; labels, children/children with the finding. D, first echocardiogram below the aneurysm threshold (maximal Z < 2.5) after discharge in children whose largest in-hospital finding was an aneurysm and who had at least 1 echocardiogram after discharge (small n = 218, medium n = 76, and large or giant n = 47); 1 − Kaplan–Meier, time 0 = discharge, follow-up censored at the last echocardiogram and at 1,095 days; shaded bands, 95% CIs. At 1 year: small 89.5% (84.6–93.4), medium 53.0% (41.1–66.0), and large or giant 16.7% (7.8–33.9); log-rank across sizes P < 0.001. A normal lumen (maximal Z < 2) was reached by 1 year in 86.8% (81.3–91.4), 45.7% (34.2–59.0), and 5.9% (1.5–22.1), and by 2 years in 88.0% (82.4–92.5), 57.9% (44.9–71.6), and 9.8% (3.2–28.1), respectively. Because an event is dated at the echocardiogram that showed it, early regression partly reflects when the first follow-up echocardiogram was done. Any involvement, Z ≥ 2; aneurysm, Z ≥ 2.5; medium or larger, Z ≥ 5.

Figure 5. Risk factors for an aneurysm after discharge and risk-based selection for the first post-discharge echocardiogram. A–D, aneurysm (maximal Z ≥ 2.5) at the first echocardiogram 1–30 days after discharge in children discharged with a maximal in-hospital Z score below 2.5 (derivation n = 1,702, 33 aneurysms; validation n = 636, 8 aneurysms). A, by number of risk factors (age <12 months, IVIG resistance, in-hospital dilation only [Z 2 to <2.5], and discharge CRP >20 mg/L or white blood cell count >12 × 10⁹/L); blue, derivation; red, validation. B, by the exact in-hospital maximal Z score, in the 358 derivation children whose in-hospital maximum was reported as an exact Z value; Cochran–Armitage test for trend P = 0.020; Firth odds ratio per 0.5 Z, 1.39 (95% CI, 1.06–1.84), and 1.34 (1.02–1.76) adjusted for age <12 months and IVIG resistance. C, by age at admission (derivation), after an uncomplicated in-hospital course (in-hospital Z < 2) and in all children discharged with a maximal Z below 2.5; infants younger than 3 months: 0/26 and 0/27. Whiskers in A–C, Wilson 95% CIs; labels, children with an aneurysm/children. D, echocardiograms avoided and aneurysms detected (sensitivity) if the first post-discharge echocardiogram were limited to children meeting each rule (all children; at least 1 risk factor; at least 2 risk factors; or in-hospital dilation only or IVIG resistance, open diamond); whiskers, bootstrap 95% CIs (derivation) and the Wilson 95% CI of the detected proportion (validation); labels, aneurysms detected/aneurysms. With the ≥1-risk-factor rule, 5 aneurysms were missed in the derivation cohort (3 small and 2 medium). E and F, cumulative incidence of a newly detected aneurysm after discharge (1 − Kaplan–Meier; derivation; echocardiograms to February 28, 2025; time 0 = discharge; an aneurysm is dated at the first echocardiogram that showed it; follow-up censored at the last echocardiogram and at 730 days; shaded bands, 95% CIs). E, children with an uncomplicated in-hospital course and at least 1 echocardiogram after discharge (n = 1,885) by number of risk factors (age <12 months, IVIG resistance, and discharge CRP >20 mg/L or white blood cell count >12 × 10⁹/L): at 1 year 0 factors 1.3% (0.7–2.7), 1 factor 2.0% (1.1–3.5), and ≥2 factors 4.7% (2.5–8.6); log-rank P = 0.002. F, children whose in-hospital maximum was dilation only (n = 93) compared with an uncomplicated course (in-hospital Z < 2; n = 1,885): at 1 year 9.2% (4.7–17.5) versus 2.1% (1.5–3.1); log-rank P < 0.001. CRP, C-reactive protein; IVIG, intravenous immunoglobulin.
